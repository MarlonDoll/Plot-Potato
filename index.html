    What is the Window of Tolerance?

    Your nervous system has zones

    Developed by psychiatrist Dr Dan Siegel, the Window of Tolerance describes the optimal zone where your nervous system is regulated — you can think clearly, feel your emotions, connect with others, and respond (rather than react) to life.

    When stress or trauma pushes you outside this window, your automatic survival responses take over. Understanding which zone you're in is the first step to self-regulation.

    The three zones

    Hyperarousal — Fight or Flight

    Your sympathetic nervous system is highly activated. The brain perceives threat and mobilises energy.

    • Anxiety, panic, racing heart
    • Anger, irritability, rage
    • Racing or intrusive thoughts
    • Hypervigilance — scanning for danger
    • Overwhelm, feeling out of control
    • Physical tension, trembling

    Window of Tolerance — Optimal Zone

    Your nervous system is regulated. You have access to your full emotional and cognitive range.

    • Calm, grounded, present
    • Curious, engaged, open
    • Able to feel and process emotions
    • Connected to self and others
    • Flexible thinking, good problem-solving

    Hypoarousal — Freeze or Collapse

    Your parasympathetic nervous system has shut things down. The brain conserves energy in the face of perceived helplessness.

    • Numbness, emptiness, disconnection
    • Fatigue, heaviness, inability to move
    • Brain fog, difficulty thinking
    • Dissociation — feeling unreal
    • Depression, hopelessness
    • Feeling invisible or absent
    Growing your window

    The window can expand

    Through therapy, consistent self-regulation practice, and safe relationships, your window of tolerance can widen over time. This means you can handle more stress without being pushed outside your zone.

    Key practices that grow the window:

    • Regular mindfulness and body awareness
    • Somatic (body-based) therapy or movement
    • Safe social connection and co-regulation
    • Consistent sleep, nutrition and exercise
    • Trauma-informed therapy (EMDR, SE, IFS, etc.)
    • Gentle titration — gradually approaching difficult feelings
